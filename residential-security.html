The First Person Residents See

Professional At The Front. Aware Of Everything Behind It.

Residential personnel can become part of the everyday experience of a development. Residents should recognise them, visitors should know where to go and management should know there is a dependable presence on site.

Blackstar starts by understanding how the building actually works. That can include entrances, resident access, reception areas, visitor arrangements, contractors, deliveries, communal spaces, car parks, management procedures and the times when the development is busiest.

The role can then be defined around the character of the property. In some developments that may be primarily front-of-house. In others, appropriately licensed security responsibilities may form a much larger part of the requirement.

01

A Professional Welcome

Residents, guests and contractors have a visible person available at the point where they enter the development.

02

Residents Feel Looked After

A consistent on-site presence gives residents a clear point of contact for everyday building and access concerns.

03

Better Control Of Access

Visitor, contractor and delivery arrangements can be supported according to the procedures set by management.

04

Discreet Security Awareness

Where security duties are included, personnel remain alert to what is happening without making the development feel unnecessarily guarded.

Concierge Or Security?

Same Entrance. Different Responsibilities.

Two people may appear to occupy a similar front-of-house position while carrying very different responsibilities. What the person actually does determines whether the role is concierge, security or a combination that requires appropriately licensed personnel.

01

Concierge

Resident assistance, visitor direction, front-of-house enquiries and other administrative building responsibilities.

02

Security

Where personnel are contracted to perform licensable private security activity, the appropriate SIA licence is required.

03

Combined Role

A development may want a professional front-of-house presence that also carries clearly defined security responsibilities.

04

Defined Before Deployment

Responsibilities should be established before personnel are appointed so the correct role and licensing can be used.

Residential Concierge FAQs

What Property Managers Need To Know Before Appointing Cover.

Clear answers covering concierge duties, residential security, SIA licensing, visitors, access, staffing and ongoing cover.

The role depends on the development. Responsibilities can include front-of-house presence, resident assistance, visitor direction, supporting access procedures and communicating building issues to the appropriate management team.

Concierge duties are generally front-of-house and service-focused. Residential security involves defined security responsibilities. Where contracted personnel carry out licensable private security activity, the appropriate SIA licence is required.

The title alone does not determine whether a licence is required. The actual duties matter. If contracted personnel carry out licensable security activity, the appropriate SIA licence is required.

Visitor and contractor procedures can form part of the role where agreed with management. Personnel should be briefed on how different visitors are expected to enter and move through the development.

Overnight and extended-hour requirements can be discussed. The role, required duties, shift structure and personnel availability need to be established before cover is agreed.

Cost depends on operating hours, personnel numbers, location, responsibilities, shift pattern and whether the role contains licensable security duties. The requirement should be defined before the appropriate cover is quoted.

Delivery procedures can be included within the assignment where agreed with management. The exact responsibility should be defined as part of the building brief.

Awareness of agreed communal areas can form part of a residential assignment. Any security, patrol or CCTV responsibilities should be properly defined so the correct personnel and licensing are used.

Residential personnel can work alongside managing agents, property managers, facilities teams and other on-site personnel where responsibilities and communication routes are clearly defined.

Incident responsibilities should be established before deployment. Personnel can follow the agreed procedure and escalate to management, supervisors, emergency services or other appropriate parties where required.

Temporary cover can be considered alongside longer-term residential assignments, subject to the required responsibilities and personnel availability.
