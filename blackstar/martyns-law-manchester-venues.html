What Is Martyn's Law and Does It Apply to My Manchester Venue?

A practical guide to Martyn's Law for Manchester venues, including the 200-person threshold, standard and enhanced tiers, qualifying events and what businesses should prepare for before the legislation comes into force.

Martyn's Law Manchester

The Short Answer

Martyn's Law is the common name for the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025. It creates legal duties for certain publicly accessible premises and events to be prepared to protect people if a terrorist attack occurs.

For most premises, the law can apply where it is reasonable to expect 200 or more people to be present at the same time from time to time. That number includes staff, not only customers or ticket holders.

Premises expecting 200 to 799 people generally fall into the standard tier. Premises expecting 800 or more people generally fall into the enhanced tier, subject to the detailed criteria and exceptions in the Act.

As of August 2026, the substantive requirements of Martyn's Law are not yet in force. The Security Industry Authority currently expects the legislation to come into force in spring 2027.

Manchester businesses do not need to wait until then to start understanding their premises, identifying who is responsible and considering how evacuation, invacuation, lockdown and communication would work if a serious incident occurred.

The Legislation

What Is Martyn's Law?

Martyn's Law is the commonly used name for the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025. Its purpose is to improve protective security and preparedness at certain premises and events across the United Kingdom.

The law is named in memory of Martyn Hett, one of the 22 people killed in the Manchester Arena terrorist attack in 2017.

The legislation creates a tiered system so that the duties placed on a venue are proportionate to its size and circumstances. Larger premises and qualifying events will be expected to do more than smaller standard-tier premises.

Why It Matters Here

Why Is Martyn's Law Particularly Significant for Manchester?

Martyn's Law has a direct connection to Manchester. It followed years of campaigning by Martyn Hett's mother, Figen Murray, after the Manchester Arena attack.

For businesses across Manchester's night-time economy, events sector, retail areas and visitor economy, the legislation now creates a clear framework for thinking about how people would be protected during a terrorist incident.

Manchester City Council has also introduced Martyn's Law model licensing conditions for licensed premises and encourages businesses to consider protective-security measures ahead of the national legislation coming fully into force.

Check Your Venue

Does Martyn's Law Apply to My Venue?

A premises is likely to fall within Martyn's Law where all of the following conditions are met.

1. The Premises Includes a Building

For qualifying premises, the location must include a building or part of a building. The rules for qualifying events are broader and can also cover land.

2. It Is Mainly Used for a Purpose Covered by the Act

Schedule 1 identifies the types of publicly accessible activities and premises that can fall within scope.

3. 200 or More People Can Reasonably Be Expected

It must be reasonable to expect that at least 200 individuals, including staff, may be present at the same time from time to time.

4. The Premises Is Not Excluded

The Act contains exclusions and special rules for particular types of premises, so meeting the attendance threshold alone does not automatically mean a premises falls within scope.

Manchester Businesses

What Types of Manchester Venues Can Be Covered by Martyn's Law?

The legislation covers a broad range of uses. Depending on the other qualifying criteria, Manchester venues affected by Martyn's Law can include:

  • Bars and pubs
  • Nightclubs
  • Restaurants, cafés and food halls
  • Hotels
  • Shops and shopping centres
  • Theatres and cinemas
  • Concert halls and arenas
  • Gyms and leisure centres
  • Visitor attractions
  • Certain public events

200–799 People

What Are the Martyn's Law Standard Tier Requirements?

A qualifying premises where between 200 and 799 people can reasonably be expected to be present at the same time generally falls into the standard tier.

Once the relevant requirements come into force, the responsible person will need to notify the SIA and ensure appropriate public protection procedures are in place, so far as reasonably practicable.

The Four Public Protection Procedures

Evacuation

How people can be moved out of the premises or away from danger safely.

Invacuation

How people can be moved to a safer place inside the premises when remaining inside presents less risk.

Lockdown

How the premises can be secured to restrict movement into or out of the location when necessary.

Communication

How people at the premises will be warned about danger and given clear instructions.

An Important Distinction

Does the Standard Tier Require CCTV, Barriers or Security Guards?

No, not automatically.

The Government describes standard-tier requirements as being focused on practical procedures and staff preparedness. The legislation does not automatically require a standard-tier premises to install new physical security equipment or make building alterations simply because it falls within the tier.

Equally, Martyn's Law does not create a universal rule that every qualifying premises must hire security guards. The procedures chosen need to be appropriate and reasonably practicable for the individual premises.

800+ People

What Are the Martyn's Law Enhanced Tier Requirements?

Qualifying premises where 800 or more individuals can reasonably be expected to be present at the same time will generally fall into the enhanced tier.

Enhanced-tier premises must have the same four public protection procedures as the standard tier, but must also consider appropriate public protection measures so far as reasonably practicable.

Enhanced-Tier Measures Fall Into Four Areas

Monitoring

Measures for monitoring the premises, event and immediate vicinity where appropriate, potentially using existing systems such as CCTV.

Movement

Measures relating to how people enter, leave and move around the premises or event.

Physical Security

Appropriate measures connected with the physical safety and security of the premises or event.

Security of Information

Measures to prevent sensitive information about the premises, its layout, operation or security arrangements being unnecessarily accessible to somebody who could use it to plan an attack.

Proportionate Protection

What Does "Reasonably Practicable" Mean Under Martyn's Law?

Martyn's Law does not assume that every venue should implement exactly the same security measures.

The official guidance explains reasonably practicable as balancing what can be done to achieve the public-protection objective against the cost, time and difficulty involved.

That means the appropriate approach for a 250-person restaurant may look very different from the approach required at a major arena or 5,000-person event.

The 200 & 800 Thresholds

How Do I Work Out How Many People My Venue Has for Martyn's Law?

The thresholds are based on the number of people it is reasonable to expect will be present at the same time, not simply the number of customers you see on an average day.

Staff are included. Depending on the premises, relevant evidence could include attendance records, ticketing data, seating arrangements, occupancy information and other reliable information about how the premises normally operates.

The phrase "from time to time" is important. A premises can still fall within scope even if it normally has fewer than 200 people, where reaching 200 or more is something that can reasonably be expected to happen occasionally.

Example

A Manchester venue that normally has 120 people inside but predictably reaches 250 on certain weekend nights may need to consider those busier periods when deciding whether the premises falls within scope.

Manchester Events

Does Martyn's Law Apply to Events in Manchester?

Yes. Certain events can fall directly within Martyn's Law even where the underlying premises is not already an enhanced-tier premises.

A qualifying event generally needs to meet all of the relevant statutory criteria, including:

  • The event is accessible to members of the public
  • Entry controls check for a ticket, pass, membership or guest status
  • 800 or more people, including staff, can reasonably be expected at the same time
  • The event is not already taking place at enhanced-tier premises
  • The event is not otherwise excluded under the Act

Qualifying events are subject to the enhanced-tier requirements.

Below 800 Attendees

Does Martyn's Law Apply to Events With Fewer Than 800 People?

An event with fewer than 800 individuals at the same time will not itself qualify as a qualifying event under the event provisions of the Act.

However, it may still take place inside premises that independently falls within the standard or enhanced tier. In that situation, the responsibilities attached to the qualifying premises still need to be considered.

Legal Responsibility

Who Is Responsible for Martyn's Law Compliance?

Every qualifying premises or qualifying event has a responsible person.

For premises, this is generally the individual, organisation or company with control of the premises for the activity that brings it within scope.

For qualifying events, it is generally the person or organisation with control of the premises in connection with its use for the event.

Important

You Cannot Outsource the Legal Responsibility

The responsible person can delegate individual tasks and obtain outside support, but cannot transfer the overall legal responsibility to a security company, consultant or other contractor.

Security & Martyn's Law

Does Martyn's Law Mean I Have to Hire Security Guards?

No. Martyn's Law does not create a blanket rule requiring every qualifying venue to employ private security.

The Government has deliberately designed the legislation so that businesses can understand and fulfil their duties without automatically needing to purchase specialist third-party services.

However, professional security may form part of the procedures or measures a responsible person decides are appropriate for their particular premises or event.

For example, an enhanced-tier venue might consider monitoring, access control, searching, movement of people, perimeter arrangements or security staffing as part of its wider protective-security system where those measures are appropriate and reasonably practicable.

Avoid a Common Misunderstanding

Does Hiring a Security Company Make My Venue Martyn's Law Compliant?

No. Hiring a security company does not automatically make a venue compliant with Martyn's Law.

Security personnel can potentially support parts of a venue's chosen arrangements, but the legislation covers much more than having guards at the entrance.

Procedures, communication, staff awareness, management responsibility, movement, physical security and other relevant measures need to work together around the actual premises or event.

Staff Preparedness

Does Martyn's Law Require Counter-Terrorism Training?

Staff who have responsibilities within a venue's public protection procedures need to understand the relevant procedures and how to implement them.

That does not mean every venue must buy an expensive external Martyn's Law training course. Government guidance expressly states that paying for third-party training is not mandatory.

Training and instruction can take different forms, including briefings, induction, e-learning, supervised practice and other methods suited to the premises.

Free counter-terrorism awareness resources are also available through ProtectUK, including ACT Awareness learning.

Existing Security Teams

Are SIA Licensed Security Staff Automatically Prepared for Martyn's Law?

Not necessarily.

The Home Office guidance specifically notes that even staff who already have security training, including training for an SIA licence, are still likely to need site-specific instruction on the procedures and measures applying at the individual premises or event.

A security officer needs to know much more than generic counter-terrorism principles. They need to understand what this venue will do, who makes decisions, where people move and what their own role is when a procedure is activated.

Manchester City Council

What Is Manchester Already Doing About Martyn's Law?

Manchester City Council has already incorporated protective-security thinking into its licensing guidance.

The Council publishes Martyn's Law model licensing conditions for pubs, bars, restaurants and other licensed premises and has encouraged licensed businesses to adopt protective measures ahead of national commencement.

Manchester also works with Counter Terrorism Policing and CityCo to provide ACT training opportunities for licensed businesses, with free online ACT Awareness learning also available.

Check Your Existing Premises Licence Too

Martyn's Law is not the only security consideration for a Manchester venue. Existing premises-licence conditions concerning door supervision, searches, CCTV or other operational requirements continue to matter separately.

Preparing for Spring 2027

How Can a Manchester Venue Prepare for Martyn's Law Now?

Although the substantive duties are not yet in force, venues can use the implementation period to understand where they are likely to sit and identify gaps in their current preparedness.

1. Determine Whether Your Premises Is Likely to Be in Scope

Look at how the premises is used, the number of people reasonably expected at the same time and any exclusions or special provisions that may apply.

2. Identify the Responsible Person

Establish which individual, company or organisation has legal control of the premises for the relevant use.

3. Review Your Existing Emergency Procedures

Look at how evacuation, invacuation, lockdown and communication could operate using the systems, staff and premises you already have.

4. Make Sure Staff Understand Their Roles

A written plan has little value if the people expected to implement it do not understand what to do.

5. Review Existing Security Arrangements

Enhanced-tier premises in particular should consider how existing CCTV, entrances, access control, staffing, physical security and movement arrangements contribute to the wider protective-security system.

6. Use Current Official Guidance

The Home Office has already published statutory guidance, while the SIA and ProtectUK provide additional information for premises and events preparing for commencement.

Where Security Fits

How Can Professional Security Support a Manchester Venue?

Professional security can support parts of a venue's wider protective-security arrangements where the responsible person determines that security personnel are appropriate.

Depending on the environment, that could include:

  • Access control and managing entrances
  • Monitoring customer and crowd movement
  • Supporting appropriate searching procedures
  • Monitoring the premises and relevant surrounding areas
  • Supporting movement during an emergency
  • Communicating with management and other staff
  • Providing a trained security presence where the venue's assessment calls for one
