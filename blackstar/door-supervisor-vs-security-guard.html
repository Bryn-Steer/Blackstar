Door Supervisor vs Security Guard: What's the Difference?

The difference between a door supervisor and security guard, how their SIA licences differ, where each can work and which type of security your venue, event or business may need.

Understand the Difference

The Short Answer

The main difference between a door supervisor and a security guard is the type of security work their SIA licence allows them to perform, particularly at licensed premises.

An appropriately licensed door supervisor can carry out door-supervision activity at licensed premises and can also perform the licensable activity of a security guard. A Security Guard licence does not allow somebody to perform licensable door-supervision duties at licensed premises.

In practical terms, door supervisors are often associated with pubs, bars, nightclubs, entertainment venues and events, while security guards are commonly used for offices, commercial premises, property, retail environments and other guarding assignments.

For a business hiring security, the important question is therefore not simply "Do I need a guard?" but "What security activities need to be carried out, and which licence is appropriate for them?"

Door Supervision

What Is a Door Supervisor?

A door supervisor is a security professional licensed to carry out relevant security activities at licensed premises.

In SIA licensing terms, this can include guarding licensed premises against unauthorised access, damage, theft or disorderly behaviour.

Although the name suggests somebody standing at a door, the role can extend well beyond the entrance. Door supervisors may work throughout a venue depending on the security plan, licence conditions and duties required.

What Does a Door Supervisor Do?

  • Help protect licensed premises against unauthorised access
  • Support controlled entry and venue access procedures
  • Manage queues and busy entrance areas where required
  • Monitor behaviour and identify developing problems
  • Respond appropriately to disorder and security incidents
  • Support venue rules and agreed entry procedures
  • Help protect customers, staff and the premises
Security Guarding

What Is a Security Guard?

A security guard provides a physical security presence to help protect premises and property against risks such as unauthorised access, theft or damage.

Contracted security guards are commonly deployed across offices, commercial premises, retail environments, industrial sites, residential property and other locations requiring a visible and accountable security presence.

What Does a Security Guard Do?

  • Protect property and premises
  • Monitor entrances and access points
  • Help prevent unauthorised access
  • Patrol premises where required
  • Observe and report suspicious activity
  • Respond according to agreed site procedures
  • Provide a visible security presence for staff and visitors
The Key Difference

Why Do Licensed Premises Need Door Supervisors Rather Than Security Guards?

This is where the difference between a Door Supervisor licence and Security Guard licence becomes particularly important.

The SIA states that a Door Supervisor licence is required for relevant guarding activity at licensed premises, subject to the applicable licensing rules and exemptions.

This generally covers environments where the premises is licensed for activities such as selling alcohol for consumption on the premises or providing relevant regulated entertainment.

A standard Security Guard licence does not simply become a Door Supervisor licence because the operative is placed outside a bar or nightclub. The licence has to match the licensable activity being performed.

Licence Scope

Can a Door Supervisor Work as a Security Guard?

Yes

A Door Supervisor Licence Also Covers Security Guarding

The SIA states that a Door Supervisor licence also permits the holder to perform the licensable activity of a security guard.

That means an appropriately licensed door supervisor can potentially be deployed on security-guarding assignments as well as relevant door-supervision work.

The Reverse Does Not Apply

Can a Security Guard Work as a Door Supervisor?

Not on a Security Guard Licence Alone

Security Guarding Does Not Automatically Cover Door Supervision

Someone holding only a Security Guard licence cannot simply perform licensable door-supervision activity that requires a Door Supervisor licence.

This distinction matters when businesses hire outsourced security for bars, nightclubs, licensed events and other environments where door-supervision activity is required.

SIA Licensing

Do Door Supervisors and Security Guards Need an SIA Licence?

In the UK, whether an individual requires an SIA licence depends on the security activity being carried out and the employment arrangement.

Contracted frontline security personnel carrying out licensable security activity generally need the appropriate SIA licence.

There is an important distinction for in-house security. Many directly employed in-house security-guarding roles do not ordinarily require an SIA licence, whereas door supervision at licensed premises remains licensable even when performed in-house.

Businesses can check an individual's licence using the official SIA public register.

Different Qualifications

Is Door Supervisor Training Different from Security Guard Training?

Yes. The SIA recognises separate licence-linked qualifications for Door Supervision and Security Guarding.

As of 2026, applicants for either front-line licence must meet the relevant SIA training requirements before applying. The current qualification for door supervision is different from the qualification for security guarding.

The SIA also now requires relevant refresher qualifications for existing Door Supervisor and Security Guard licence holders when renewing, subject to its current renewal rules.

Choosing the Right Security

Do I Need a Door Supervisor or Security Guard?

The answer depends on the premises and the security activity you need somebody to perform.

Bars, Pubs & Nightclubs

Where relevant security activity is being carried out at licensed premises, appropriately licensed door supervisors are likely to be the relevant security personnel. The premises licence may also contain specific conditions governing their deployment.

Events

Event security can involve different roles. An event may require door supervisors, security guards and non-security stewards depending on the venue, licensing and duties being carried out.

Commercial Premises

Offices, commercial properties, warehouses and similar environments may be better suited to security guarding or manned guarding where the primary requirement is protecting premises, property and access.

Explore Manned Guarding

Customer-Facing Venues

The correct licence is essential, but the person also needs to suit the environment. At a customer-facing venue, security can be one of the first parts of the business a guest encounters, so professionalism, communication and presentation matter alongside security capability.

Manchester Venues

Door Supervisors for Licensed Premises in Manchester

Businesses looking for door supervisors in Manchester should check the conditions attached to their individual premises licence rather than assuming that every venue follows the same security arrangement.

Manchester City Council's published model licensing conditions allow conditions to specify when door supervisors must work, the number required, where they must be positioned or a ratio of door supervisors to customers.

Its model conditions also identify factors including venue size, expected attendance, event type, location, time of year, special occasions and premises-licence conditions when assessing door-supervision requirements.

The important point is that the security team should be built around the venue, not copied from a generic staffing formula.

Hiring Security

Hiring Door Supervisors or Security Guards in Manchester

If you are hiring SIA licensed security in Manchester, start by explaining the environment and duties rather than simply requesting a particular job title.

A professional security company should establish:

  • Whether the premises is licensed
  • What security activities need to be performed
  • Which SIA licence is appropriate for those duties
  • How many security personnel are required
  • Where security personnel need to be positioned
  • What the premises licence requires
  • What kind of people best fit the environment
