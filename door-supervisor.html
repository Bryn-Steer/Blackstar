What Door Supervision Actually Means

It Starts At The Door. It Doesn't End There.

A professional door supervisor is not simply there to stand at the entrance. The role can involve controlling access, protecting people, identifying developing problems and helping the venue operate safely throughout the night.

01 · ENTRY

Controlling Who Comes In

The entrance is the first point of control. Door supervisors help manage access before potential problems are allowed into the venue.

  • Managing customer entry
  • Supporting age and identification checks
  • Managing guest lists or ticket requirements
  • Refusing entry professionally where required
02 · AWARENESS

Seeing Problems Before They Grow

Strong door supervision is proactive. Personnel should remain aware of behaviour, crowd pressure and changes in the atmosphere before intervention becomes more difficult.

  • Monitoring customer behaviour
  • Recognising signs of intoxication
  • Identifying developing conflict
  • Communicating concerns early
03 · COMMUNICATION

Managing People, Not Just Incidents

Many situations can be resolved before they become confrontational. How a door supervisor speaks to someone can be as important as when they intervene.

  • Clear customer communication
  • De-escalating difficult situations
  • Explaining venue decisions professionally
  • Supporting venue staff during disputes
04 · INSIDE THE VENUE

The Job Continues Inside

Door supervision is not necessarily limited to the entrance. Depending on the assignment, supervisors may support security throughout the venue.

  • Monitoring busy areas
  • Supporting bar and venue staff
  • Responding to incidents
  • Managing restricted areas
05 · RESPONSE

Responding When Something Happens

When an incident cannot be prevented, the priority becomes controlling the situation, protecting people and communicating clearly with the venue.

  • Conflict management
  • Removing individuals where appropriate
  • Escalating serious incidents
  • Recording relevant incident information
06 · CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

Protecting The Venue's Reputation

Your door team can be the first people customers meet and the last people they see. Their behaviour becomes part of how people judge your venue.

  • Professional first impressions
  • Respectful customer treatment
  • Consistent venue standards
  • Security without damaging the atmosphere
