Clear Guidance Throughout

Helping People Know Where To Go Next.

Good stewarding removes uncertainty. Guests should know where to enter, where to sit, where facilities are and how to move through the event without unnecessary confusion.

Blackstar starts by understanding the event, venue layout, expected attendance, entrances, seating areas, public routes, accessibility requirements, restricted zones and the responsibilities already held by organisers and security personnel.

Stewards can then be positioned around the actual guest journey, helping people move confidently through the event while providing organisers with additional eyes, information and public-facing support.

What Event Stewards Actually Do

The Guest Journey From Arrival To Exit.

Stewarding supports the practical movement and experience of people throughout an event. Different positions solve different problems at different stages.

01 · ARRIVAL

Welcoming & Directing Guests

Arrival can be one of the busiest stages of an event. Stewards help people identify the correct route before confusion begins to build.

  • Entrance directions
  • Queue information
  • Ticket-route guidance
  • General guest assistance
02 · WAYFINDING

Helping People Find Their Way

Large venues and unfamiliar environments become easier to navigate when visible personnel can give guests clear, consistent directions.

  • Seating directions
  • Facility information
  • Zone guidance
  • Exit information
03 · MOVEMENT

Supporting Crowd Movement

Stewards can support the movement of people through public areas by observing changing conditions and guiding guests towards the correct routes.

  • High-footfall areas
  • Queue direction
  • Walkway support
  • Reporting developing congestion
04 · SEATING

Supporting Seating & Zones

In seated and zoned events, clear stewarding helps guests reach the correct area while reducing unnecessary movement around the venue.

  • Block and section guidance
  • Row and seat assistance
  • Zone information
  • Accessibility assistance
05 · AWARENESS

Observing & Reporting

Stewards provide an additional visible presence across the event and can report developing issues through agreed communication routes.

  • Observing public areas
  • Reporting hazards
  • Raising guest concerns
  • Escalating to supervisors
06 · DEPARTURE

Helping People Leave Clearly

Large numbers of guests can leave at the same time. Stewarding remains important after the main event has finished.

  • Exit guidance
  • Route information
  • Transport directions
  • Departure assistance
Stewarding Or Security?

Similar Environment. Different Responsibilities.

Stewarding and security frequently operate alongside one another, but they are not interchangeable. The duties assigned to each role matter.

01

Stewarding

Wayfinding, guest information, seating assistance, movement support and other genuine non-security event duties.

02

Security

Where personnel carry out licensable private security activity, appropriately licensed personnel are required.

03

Combined Deployment

Many events require both. The important part is deciding which positions need stewarding and which require security.

04

Clear Escalation

Stewards should know who to contact when a situation moves beyond their assigned responsibility.

Event Stewarding FAQs

What Organisers Need To Know Before Event Day.

Clear answers covering stewarding duties, staffing, security, SIA licensing, event planning and guest movement.

Event stewards can support wayfinding, seating, guest information, public movement, queue direction, observation and other appropriate event-support duties.

A steward primarily supports the event operation and guest experience. Security personnel can perform security duties that may amount to licensable activity. The actual responsibilities assigned to each person determine the appropriate role.

Genuine stewarding duties can fall outside SIA licensing. If personnel perform licensable private security activity, the appropriate SIA licence is required. Duties therefore need to be defined correctly.

There is no single staffing number that suits every event. Attendance, venue layout, entrances, seating, zones, event format, guest movement, accessibility and the responsibilities assigned to stewards can all affect the requirement.

Stewards can support public movement by providing directions, monitoring designated areas and reporting developing congestion through the agreed event communication structure.

Yes. Seating, section and zone assistance is a common guest-facing stewarding function at seated events and larger venues.

Requirements involving both stewarding and appropriately licensed security personnel can be discussed. Roles should be assigned according to the actual responsibilities being performed.

Useful information includes the event date, location, operating hours, expected attendance, venue layout, entrances, seating areas, zones, guest routes and the duties you expect personnel to perform.

Yes. Stewarding personnel can work alongside organisers, venue employees, event control, security teams and other operational personnel where responsibilities and communication routes are clearly established.

Multi-day and larger event requirements can be discussed. Staffing, operating hours, shift requirements and the responsibilities across each day need to be understood first.

Communication and escalation procedures should be agreed before the event. Where a situation requires security intervention, the matter can then be escalated to the appropriate personnel.

Short-notice requirements can be considered subject to suitable personnel availability and enough information being available to assess and brief the event.
