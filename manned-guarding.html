More Than A Uniform On Site

Protection Built Around The Way Your Site Works.

A security guard is most effective when they understand what they are protecting, who should be there and what is expected when something does not look right.

Blackstar starts by understanding the premises. That can include operating hours, entrances, visitor procedures, staff movement, valuable assets, restricted areas, delivery points, patrol routes and known operational concerns.

The assignment can then be built around the actual site rather than simply placing somebody at a desk or entrance. Personnel know where they should be, what they should monitor and how concerns should be reported or escalated.

01

A Reassuring Visible Presence

A professional security presence can reassure employees, visitors and contractors while helping discourage unwanted activity.

02

Better Control Of The Site

Access points, visitor procedures, restricted areas and agreed patrol routes can be monitored consistently.

03

Problems Identified Earlier

Regular observation helps unusual activity, access concerns and operational issues get noticed rather than ignored.

04

Clear Accountability

Responsibilities, reporting and escalation routes are agreed so the guard knows what to do when something happens.

What Manned Guarding Actually Covers

Six Parts Of A Proper Site Presence.

Good guarding is active rather than passive. Personnel should understand the site, remain observant and know exactly what responsibility they hold throughout the assignment.

01 · ACCESS

Controlling Access

A site is easier to protect when there is clarity around who should be entering, where they should go and which areas are restricted.

  • Entrance monitoring
  • Visitor procedures
  • Contractor access
  • Restricted-area awareness
02 · PRESENCE

Maintaining A Visible Presence

Security personnel provide a clear point of accountability while demonstrating that the premises are being actively monitored.

  • Visible site presence
  • Entrance positioning
  • Staff reassurance
  • Professional representation
03 · PATROLS

Checking More Than One Position

Where agreed, patrols can help extend the security presence beyond a single entrance or reception position.

  • Agreed patrol routes
  • Perimeter awareness
  • Internal areas
  • Unusual activity reporting
04 · AWARENESS

Knowing What Looks Out Of Place

A well-briefed guard knows what normal activity looks like and is better positioned to recognise when something changes.

  • Monitoring site activity
  • Identifying unusual behaviour
  • Watching vulnerable areas
  • Raising concerns early
05 · RESPONSE

Responding When Something Happens

When an issue develops, clear procedures help the guard respond appropriately and involve the right people without unnecessary delay.

  • Incident response
  • Supervisor escalation
  • Management communication
  • Emergency procedure support
06 · REPORTING

Leaving A Clear Record

Security becomes more useful when relevant events and unusual activity are communicated rather than disappearing at the end of a shift.

  • Incident information
  • Unusual activity
  • Site concerns
  • Shift handover information

03

Access Support

Visitors, contractors, deliveries and restricted areas can be managed around agreed client procedures.

04

Combined Approach

Larger sites may benefit from multiple positions, patrols and supervisor responsibilities working together.

Manned Guarding FAQs

What Businesses Need To Know Before Appointing Guards.

Clear answers covering responsibilities, SIA licensing, staffing, patrols, access, cost and ongoing security cover.

Manned guarding places security personnel at a premises or site to carry out agreed security responsibilities. Duties can include access control, visible presence, patrols, observation, incident response and reporting depending on the assignment.

Where an individual performs licensable private security activity they must hold the appropriate SIA licence. The actual duties being performed determine which licensing requirements apply.

The appropriate role depends on the duties and environment. Door supervision is associated with licensable activity at licensed premises, while security guarding commonly applies to commercial sites, property and other guarding environments.

There is no universal number. Site size, entrances, operating hours, patrol requirements, visitor activity, assets, restricted areas and the responsibilities assigned to security can all affect staffing.

Patrol responsibilities can form part of a guarding assignment where they are appropriate for the site and clearly included within the security brief.

Cost depends on the number of personnel, hours, location, assignment duration, duties, operating conditions and notice provided. The requirement should be understood before the correct deployment is quoted.

Visitor and contractor procedures can form part of the assignment where agreed with the client. Guards should be briefed on access procedures before deployment.

Requirements covering overnight or extended operating hours can be discussed. Availability, shift structure, site responsibilities and the required level of cover need to be established first.

Yes. Contract security can operate alongside facilities management, reception, site management, operations teams and existing internal personnel where responsibilities are clearly defined.

Reporting arrangements can be defined as part of the assignment so relevant incidents, unusual activity and operational concerns are communicated through the agreed process.

One-off and temporary guarding requirements can be considered as well as ongoing assignments, subject to the site requirement and personnel availability.

Short-notice requirements can be considered subject to suitable personnel availability and sufficient information being available to assess and brief the site.
