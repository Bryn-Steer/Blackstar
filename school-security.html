Protection Without Intimidation

Safe Should Still Feel Like School.

School security needs a different approach. Personnel may be protecting a site, but they are doing it around pupils, parents, teachers, visitors and a working education environment.

Blackstar starts by understanding how the school already operates. That can include entrances, reception procedures, visitor arrangements, pupil movement, staff access, deliveries, restricted areas, school events and existing safeguarding and emergency procedures.

The security role can then be clearly defined around the school rather than imposed upon it. Personnel know where they should be, what they are responsible for and when an issue needs to be passed to school leadership, safeguarding staff or another appropriate service.

01

A Safer School Environment

Security supports existing measures around access, visitors, site activity and identified risks.

02

Appropriate Around Pupils

The role is delivered with awareness that a school is an education environment, not simply another guarded premises.

03

Support Staff Can Rely On

School staff have an additional professional security resource when access concerns or incidents develop.

04

Clear Responsibility

Security responsibilities and escalation routes are defined so everybody understands where the role begins and ends.

What School Security Actually Supports

Protect The Site. Respect The Environment.

School security is not about placing an intimidating presence at the entrance. It is about supporting existing procedures with clearly defined security responsibilities.

01 · ACCESS

Supporting Controlled Access

Clear entry arrangements help the school understand who is on site and direct visitors through the appropriate process.

  • Entrance monitoring
  • Visitor routes
  • Contractor access
  • Restricted areas
02 · PRESENCE

Providing A Visible Presence

Security can be visible enough to provide reassurance and accountability without dominating the atmosphere of the school.

  • Entrance presence
  • Staff reassurance
  • Site awareness
  • Professional representation
03 · VISITORS

Supporting Visitor Procedures

Visitors, contractors and other external personnel should enter through the arrangements established by the school.

  • Visitor direction
  • Reception support
  • Contractor procedures
  • Unauthorised access concerns
04 · AWARENESS

Watching What Changes

A well-briefed security officer understands normal site activity and can identify situations that require further attention.

  • Site observation
  • Boundary awareness
  • Unusual activity
  • Early escalation
05 · RESPONSE

Supporting Incident Response

Security personnel can support agreed procedures when an access, safety or security-related issue develops.

  • Incident response
  • Conflict management
  • Management escalation
  • Emergency procedure support
06 · REPORTING

Keeping Staff Informed

Relevant incidents and concerns should reach the appropriate school personnel rather than disappearing when the security shift ends.

  • Incident information
  • Access concerns
  • Unusual activity
  • Clear handovers
Security & Safeguarding

Security Supports Safeguarding. It Doesn't Replace It.

The school remains responsible for its safeguarding arrangements. Security should understand the procedures relevant to its role and know when information or concerns must be passed to the appropriate member of school staff.

01

The School

Defines safeguarding arrangements, visitor procedures, school policies and the people responsible for managing concerns.

02

Security

Carries out the agreed security role while remaining within clearly established responsibilities and procedures.

03

Communication

Security personnel know who to contact when a concern involves safeguarding, behaviour, access or another school matter.

04

Escalation

Situations are passed to school leadership, safeguarding personnel, emergency services or other appropriate parties when required.

Education Environments

Every Setting Works Differently.

01

Primary Schools

Environments where security needs to remain especially measured, professional and appropriate around younger pupils, parents and staff.

02

Secondary Schools

Larger pupil populations, multiple access points, visitor activity and busy arrival and departure periods can create different security requirements.

03

Colleges & Sixth Forms

More open education environments with greater movement between buildings, visitors, students, contractors and public-facing areas.

04

Academy Trusts

Security arrangements can be discussed across individual schools or wider groups where consistent procedures and standards are required.

05

Alternative Provision

Security requirements can be built around the specific environment, existing procedures and risks identified by the education provider.

06

School Events

Open evenings, performances, sports events and other occasions may temporarily change normal access, visitor numbers and site activity.
