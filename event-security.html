The Event Stays The Focus

Protecting The Event Without Becoming It.

Good event security should help thousands of moving parts work together without making the security operation the thing guests remember.

The requirement begins long before somebody takes a position at a gate. Blackstar starts by understanding the event, expected attendance, venue layout, arrival patterns, access points, restricted areas, audience profile and responsibilities already held by organisers, stewards and venue teams.

That allows security personnel to be positioned where they are actually useful, whether that means supporting ingress, protecting restricted areas, responding to incidents or helping maintain control as thousands of people arrive and leave.

02

Responsibilities Made Clear

What Event Security Actually Covers

One Event. Hundreds Of Moving Parts.

Event security is not one position or one task. The requirement changes from the first arrival through to the final guest leaving the site.

01 · ARRIVAL

Managing The Arrival

Large numbers of people can arrive within a very short period. The first objective is keeping that movement organised.

  • Entry-point support
  • Queue management
  • Ticket and accreditation routes
  • Monitoring crowd pressure
02 · ACCESS

Controlling Access

Different people may have permission to enter different areas. Access arrangements need to remain clear even when the site becomes busy.

  • Guest access
  • Staff and contractor access
  • Accreditation checks
  • Restricted-area control
03 · CROWD

Understanding Crowd Movement

Crowds are rarely static. People arrive, queue, gather, move between areas and leave at different stages of the event.

  • Monitoring high-footfall areas
  • Supporting safe movement
  • Identifying developing congestion
  • Communicating changing conditions
04 · PROTECTION

Protecting Restricted Areas

Backstage areas, production spaces, service routes and other controlled zones may require a different level of access from public areas.

  • Backstage access
  • Production zones
  • Staff-only areas
  • Perimeter support
05 · RESPONSE

Responding When Something Changes

Events develop in real time. Security needs clear routes for communication and escalation when an incident or operational problem occurs.

  • Incident response
  • Conflict management
  • Supervisor escalation
  • Incident information
06 · DEPARTURE

Managing The Way Out

The event is not finished when the performance, fixture or programme ends. Large-scale departures create their own pressures.

  • Exit-route support
  • Crowd dispersal
  • Transport-area awareness
  • End-of-event security

Event Security FAQs

What Organisers Need To Know Before Event Day.

Clear answers covering event staffing, licensing, stewarding, planning, access, cost and deployment.

Event security duties can include access control, restricted-area protection, appropriate searching, queue and crowd management support, incident response and working alongside organisers and venue teams. The exact requirement depends on the event.

Stewarding can involve duties such as wayfinding, seating assistance and general guest support. Security duties can include licensable guarding activity. The responsibilities assigned to each role need to be defined correctly.

Where someone carries out licensable private security activity they must hold the appropriate SIA licence. Genuine non-security stewarding duties may fall outside SIA licensing. The duties being performed determine the requirement.

There is no universal staffing ratio. Attendance, entrances, venue layout, audience profile, alcohol, restricted areas, event duration, existing stewards and assigned responsibilities can all influence staffing.

Searching may form part of an event's agreed entry and security arrangements. The procedure, responsibilities and legal requirements should be established before deployment.

Cost depends on the number and type of personnel required, event duration, location, operating hours, complexity, duties and notice provided. The event should be understood before cover is recommended and quoted.

Short-notice event requirements can be considered subject to suitable personnel availability and enough information being available to assess and brief the assignment.

Useful starting information includes the date, location, operating hours, expected attendance, venue or site plan, entrances, restricted areas, existing stewarding and the duties you expect security to perform.

Yes. Contract security can operate alongside organisers, event control, venue employees, stewards, production teams and existing in-house security. Responsibilities and communication routes should be agreed beforehand.

Multi-day and larger event requirements can be discussed. The scale, operating hours, site layout, personnel requirements and responsibilities need to be understood before a deployment is agreed.

The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 introduces preparedness requirements for certain premises and events. Organisers potentially in scope should review current Government guidance and understand their own obligations.

Individual SIA licences can be checked against the SIA register. Approved Contractors can also be checked against the SIA contractor register and company information can be independently checked through Companies House.
